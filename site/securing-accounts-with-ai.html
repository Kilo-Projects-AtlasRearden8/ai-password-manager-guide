What AI can do for your security

  • Draft strong, unique passwords — AI can suggest passphrases you can adapt
  • Spot phishing emails — paste a suspicious email and ask AI to look for red flags
  • Review privacy settings — AI can walk you through locking down your accounts
  • Explain data breach alerts — AI can help you understand what a breach means and what to do
  • Create a security plan — AI can help you make a step-by-step checklist tailored to your accounts

What AI cannot do (and should not)

  • AI cannot log into your accounts for you
  • AI cannot replace two-factor authentication
  • AI cannot see your passwords unless you give them to it (never do that)
  • AI can make mistakes — always double-check important steps
Golden rule: Never share passwords with AI
Never paste your actual passwords, recovery codes, or security keys into an AI chat. AI conversations are not a secure place for secrets. Use AI to learn and plan, not to store sensitive data.

Step-by-step: Using AI to improve your security

Step 1: Pick one account to start with

Don't try to fix everything at once. Start with your email or bank.

Step 2: Ask AI to help you review it

You can ask something like:

"I want to secure my email account. Can you walk me through turning on two-factor authentication, step by step? Keep it simple."

Or:

"I got this email that looks suspicious. Here's what it says: [paste the email text, but remove any personal info first]. Does it look like a phishing attempt?"

Step 3: Follow the steps yourself

AI gives you the plan — you do the work. Turn on 2FA, update your password, save backup codes.

Step 4: Repeat for your other accounts

Work through your list one account at a time. Use the printable checklist to track progress.

Example: Asking AI about a suspicious email

  1. Copy the email text (remove your name, address, phone number first)
  2. Paste it into an AI chat and ask: "Does this look like a phishing email? What red flags do you see?"
  3. Look for common signs: urgent language, strange sender addresses, unexpected attachments, links that don't match the company
  4. When in doubt, don't click. Go to the company's website directly by typing the address yourself.

Example: Creating a passphrase with AI

You can ask AI:

"Suggest a memorable passphrase with 5 random words for my password manager master password."

AI might suggest something like:

correct-horse-battery-staple-cloud

Use that as inspiration, then tweak it so it's unique to you. Never use an AI-suggested passphrase exactly as-is — make it your own.

Free AI tools you can use

You don't need to pay for AI to help with security:

  • Web-based AI assistants — free accounts available
  • Local AI — runs on your own computer, nothing leaves your device
  • Built-in phone AI — many phones have AI features already

Choose what you're comfortable with. You don't need AI at all to follow this guide — it's just an extra helper.

AI and privacy

  • Don't paste real passwords, account numbers, or ID numbers into AI chats
  • Remove personal details before sharing text with AI
  • If you're using a free AI tool, assume the conversation could be seen by the company running it
  • For maximum privacy, run AI locally on your own computer

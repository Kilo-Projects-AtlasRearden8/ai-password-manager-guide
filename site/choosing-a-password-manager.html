What a password manager does

  • Stores your usernames and passwords in an encrypted vault
  • Generates strong, random passwords for new accounts
  • Fills in login forms automatically on websites and apps
  • Syncs across your phone, tablet, and computer
  • Alerts you if a password has been involved in a data breach

Free vs paid

Free options work well for most people. They usually include password storage, generation, and sync across devices.

Paid options often add features like family sharing, secure file storage, emergency access, and priority support. You can start free and upgrade later.

Popular beginner-friendly choices

  • Bitwarden — open source, generous free plan, works everywhere
  • 1Password — polished apps, travel mode, family plans (paid)
  • Dashlane — easy to use, includes dark web monitoring (paid features)
  • LastPass — widely used, free tier limited to one device type
  • Apple iCloud Keychain — built into iPhone and Mac, free (Apple devices only)
  • Google Password Manager — built into Chrome and Android, free
Our recommendation for beginners: Bitwarden is a great place to start. It's open source, has a solid free plan, and works on every major device.

If you already use an iPhone or Mac, Apple iCloud Keychain is also an excellent free choice that "just works" with your devices.

Step-by-step: Get started with Bitwarden

  1. Go to bitwarden.com and create a free account
  2. Download the Bitwarden app on your phone and computer
  3. Install the Bitwarden browser extension
  4. Create a strong master password (see tips below)
  5. Save your recovery code somewhere safe (printed on paper, in a locked drawer)
  6. Add your existing logins — import from your browser or add them one at a time
  7. Turn on two-factor authentication for your Bitwarden account

Creating a strong master password

Your master password unlocks everything, so make it strong but memorable:

  • Use a passphrase: 4–6 random words strung together
  • Example: purple-train-jumps-over-moon (longer is better)
  • Avoid: birthdays, names, pet names, "password123"
  • Never reuse your master password anywhere else
  • Write it down on paper and store it somewhere safe, just in case
Important: Your recovery code
When you set up a password manager, you'll get a recovery code (sometimes called an emergency kit or secret key).
  • Print it out and store it somewhere safe
  • Never save it digitally (not in email, not in a note app)
  • If you lose your master password AND your recovery code, you may lose access to your vault

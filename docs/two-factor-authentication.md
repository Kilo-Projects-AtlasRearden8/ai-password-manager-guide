# Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Two-factor authentication adds a second lock to your account. Even if someone steals your password, they still can't get in without the second factor.

---

## What is two-factor authentication?

2FA means you need two things to log in:

1. **Something you know** — your password
2. **Something you have** — your phone, a security key, or an app code

Think of it like an ATM: you need your card (something you have) AND your PIN (something you know).

---

## Why you need it

Passwords can be guessed, stolen in data breaches, or phished. 2FA blocks most automated attacks and gives you time to change your password if it's compromised.

---

## Types of 2FA (from strongest to easiest)

| Type | How it works | Best for |
|------|--------------|----------|
| **Authenticator app** | Generates a 6-digit code that changes every 30 seconds | Most accounts |
| **Security key** | Physical USB or NFC key you tap or plug in | High-value accounts |
| **SMS text** | Codes sent by text message | Better than nothing |
| **Email code** | Codes sent to your email | Better than nothing |
| **Push notification** | Approve the login on your phone | Convenient, phone-dependent |

---

## Recommended authenticator apps

Free and reliable options:

- **Google Authenticator** — simple, works offline
- **Authy** — backs up your codes, works on multiple devices
- **Microsoft Authenticator** — simple, works offline
- **Bitwarden Authenticator** — built into Bitwarden password manager
- **Apple Passwords** — built into iPhone/iPad/Mac (iOS 18+)

> **Tip:** Use an authenticator app rather than SMS when you can. SMS codes can be intercepted by determined attackers.

---

## Step-by-step: Turn on 2FA

Here's the general process. Exact steps vary by service.

1. Log in to your account
2. Go to **Settings** → **Security** → **Two-factor authentication** (or similar)
3. Choose **Authenticator app** as your method
4. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app
5. Enter the 6-digit code the app shows you
6. Save your **backup codes** — print them or write them down

---

## Backup codes are your safety net

When you turn on 2FA, the service gives you backup codes (usually 8–10 one-time codes).

- **Print them** and store them with your important papers
- Each code works **once** — then it's used up
- If you lose your phone, you can use a backup code to get back in
- Never save backup codes in an unencrypted note or email

---

## Where to turn on 2FA first

Start with your most important accounts:

- [ ] Email (your email is the key to resetting all other passwords)
- [ ] Bank and credit card accounts
- [ ] Password manager
- [ ] Phone carrier account
- [ ] Social media accounts
- [ ] Shopping accounts (Amazon, etc.)

Most services have 2FA under Settings → Security. Search "[service name] two-factor authentication" if you can't find it.

---

## Common questions

**Q: What if I lose my phone?**
A: Use your backup codes to log in, then set up 2FA on a new device.

**Q: Do I need 2FA every time I log in?**
A: Usually no — most services remember your device for 30 days or so.

**Q: Is 2FA perfect?**
A: No, but it stops the vast majority of attacks. Combined with a password manager, you're well protected.
